# PART VI — ARCANE CHESS: THE COMPLETE GAMEPLAY SYSTEM

## 13. Core design philosophy

Arcane Chess preserves the strategic identity of chess while adding new positional abilities.

The central design rule is:

**Magic changes the geometry of the battle. Chess determines how pieces capture.**

The game must reward planning rather than uncontrolled magical attacks.

All actions are deterministic.

There are no random critical hits.

There are no random misses.

There is no damage-number system.

There are no hit points.

A normal legal capture removes the captured piece.

A magical ability may reposition pieces, but it does not directly capture an enemy.

The objective remains checkmate.

This preserves a meaningful connection to traditional chess while introducing unfamiliar tactical possibilities.

## 14. The action economy

Each player performs exactly one primary action per turn.

A primary action may be:

1. A regular legal chess move.
2. A legal magical ability.

Never permit a player to move normally and cast a spell as two separate actions during the same turn.

A magical action consumes the player's turn.

After an action:

1. Validate the resulting position.
2. Apply the action.
3. Update relevant movement flags.
4. Update special chess rights.
5. Update move counters.
6. Update mana expenditure.
7. Advance to the next player.
8. Apply the next player's turn-start mana regeneration.
9. Evaluate check, checkmate, stalemate, and other terminal conditions.
10. Update the repetition history.

Use one authoritative transition function.

Do not scatter turn advancement and mana regeneration across separate UI components.

This prevents double regeneration and state inconsistencies.

## 15. Mana system

Both sides receive a shared magical resource called **ARCANE ENERGY**.

Starting mana:

0 before the first turn-start event.

Mana regeneration:

+1 at the beginning of each player's turn.

Maximum mana:

6.

No mana is awarded for captures.

No mana is awarded for checks.

No additional mana is awarded for winning exchanges.

There is no random mana regeneration.

Unused mana carries over between turns.

Mana above the maximum is discarded.

Each player's mana is independent.

Magic costs mana immediately upon successful execution.

Illegal actions must not consume mana.

### Important implementation detail

At the initial playable position, White must have already received the first turn-start mana point.

Black receives the first mana point when Black's first turn begins.

Store the game state so that turn-start regeneration is applied exactly once.

This must remain correct after loading a saved game.

### Strategic objective

Mana should introduce meaningful opportunity costs.

The player must choose between spending energy now and saving it for later.

The game should not reward using magic simply because the player has enough mana.

# PART VII — THE SIX MAGICAL ABILITIES

## 16. Pawn — SHADOW STEP

**Cost: 2 mana**

A pawn may move exactly one square horizontally.

Allowed directions:

* One square left.
* One square right.

The destination must be empty.

The pawn cannot capture using Shadow Step.

The movement must remain within the board.

The pawn keeps its color and normal attack direction.

### Strategic purpose

Allow pawns to escape blocked structures, reshape pawn formations, and create unexpected lines.

### Special restrictions

Using Shadow Step counts as the pawn having moved.

A pawn that has used Shadow Step loses eligibility for its initial two-square advance.

Shadow Step does not grant a promotion.

The move must not leave the player's king in check.

The resulting position can create or remove ordinary chess attacks.

### Intended balance

Useful for positional restructuring.

Not an instant capture mechanism.

Not a free extra move.

---

## 17. Knight — PHASE SHIFT

**Cost: 3 mana**

A knight may teleport to an empty square directly adjacent to its current square.

Adjacency includes all eight neighboring squares.

This is different from normal knight movement.

The destination must be:

* On the board.
* Empty.
* Legal under king-safety rules.

Phase Shift cannot capture an enemy.

### Strategic purpose

Create unexpected tactical threats.

Escape poor positions.

Reposition knights into forks.

Open defensive and offensive possibilities.

### Balance considerations

The move costs a complete turn and three mana.

It must not permit a second attack or capture during the same action.

---

## 18. Bishop — GHOST WALK

**Cost: 3 mana**

A bishop may move diagonally through exactly one friendly piece.

The ability permits the bishop to ignore that single friendly blocker.

The bishop cannot pass through an enemy piece.

The bishop cannot pass through more than one occupied square.

The destination must be empty.

The bishop must remain on its original diagonal.

The destination must be different from the starting square.

### Strategic purpose

Allow bishops to escape closed structures.

Create unexpected diagonal threats.

Support long-range positional combinations.

### Balance considerations

Ghost Walk is a repositioning action only.

It never captures.

The normal bishop attack map remains unchanged after the move.

The move must not expose the friendly king to check.

---

## 19. Rook — CASTLE SWAP

**Cost: 4 mana**

A rook may exchange positions with a friendly bishop or knight.

Requirements:

* Both pieces are on the same rank or file.
* The distance between them is at most three squares.
* Every square strictly between them is empty.
* The target piece is a friendly bishop or knight.
* The resulting position is legal.

The rook and target piece swap positions simultaneously.

Neither piece captures anything.

### Strategic purpose

Reorganize defensive structures.

Move a rook into an unexpected attacking position.

Retreat or redeploy a supporting minor piece.

Create tactical combinations involving two pieces.

### Special rules

The rook is considered moved after Castle Swap.

If the rook previously retained castling rights, those rights are lost.

Swapping does not create or restore castling rights.

The swap may create check through the resulting normal attack map.

The full resulting position must be validated for king safety.

---

## 20. Queen — ROYAL COMMAND

**Cost: 5 mana**

The queen can command a nearby friendly piece to reposition.

Eligible targets:

* Pawn
* Knight
* Bishop
* Rook

The queen cannot command:

* The king.
* Another queen.
* An enemy piece.
* Herself.

The target must occupy one of the eight squares adjacent to the queen.

The destination must:

* Be empty.
* Be adjacent to the queen.
* Be no more than one square away from the target's original position, using king-style adjacency.
* Be different from the target's starting square.

The queen remains stationary.

The commanded piece moves.

Royal Command does not capture.

### Strategic purpose

Make the queen an actual battlefield commander.

Allow subtle tactical rearrangement.

Create forks, defensive formations, and discovered attacks.

Open files and diagonals.

### Pawn restrictions

A pawn relocated through Royal Command is considered moved.

Royal Command must not place a pawn directly on its promotion rank.

A pawn cannot promote through this ability.

### Rook restrictions

A rook repositioned through Royal Command loses any remaining castling eligibility.

### Balance philosophy

The queen is already the strongest traditional chess piece.

Her magical ability should create strategic opportunities rather than instantly destroy multiple enemy pieces.

---

## 21. King — ROYAL ESCAPE

**Cost: 5 mana**

**Usage limit: once per player per game.**

Royal Escape is an emergency king ability.

It can only be activated when the king is currently in check.

The king may leap exactly two squares in any of the eight directions.

Allowed vectors:

* Two horizontally.
* Two vertically.
* Two diagonally.

The king may jump over the intermediate square.

The intermediate square may be occupied.

The destination must be empty.

The destination must not be attacked under normal chess attack rules.

The king must finish in a legal position.

Royal Escape cannot capture.

### Strategic purpose

Allow a dramatic defensive response to dangerous attacks.

Create uncertainty around an otherwise forced-looking mating attack.

Encourage the attacking player to consider the opponent's mana and unused royal ability.

### Balance restrictions

Royal Escape:

* Costs five mana.
* Requires the king to be in check.
* Can only be used once.
* Consumes the entire turn.
* Cannot land on an attacked square.
* Permanently removes the king's castling rights.

### Critical rule

Royal Escape must be considered when determining checkmate.

If the defender will have five mana at the beginning of the defending turn and Royal Escape remains unused, the move generator must consider all legal escape destinations before declaring checkmate.

# PART VIII — SPECIAL INTERACTIONS AND EDGE CASES

## 22. Check detection

Check is determined using the normal attack patterns of chess pieces.

Magical abilities do not directly attack squares for the purpose of determining check.

However, magic can reposition pieces.

After a magical action, normal attack maps must be recalculated.

A resulting normal attack on the enemy king constitutes check.

## 23. Legal magical actions

An ability is legal only if:

1. The acting player owns the relevant piece.
2. The player has sufficient mana.
3. All ability-specific requirements are satisfied.
4. The target squares are valid.
5. The entire resulting position is valid.
6. The acting player's king is not in check after the action.

The final requirement applies even if the player was already in check before the action.

A magical ability may resolve check by moving the king, blocking an attack, or otherwise making the final position safe.

Do not implement special-case shortcuts that bypass normal king-safety validation.

## 24. Castling and magic

Traditional castling remains available in Arcane Chess.

However:

* A rook moved by Castle Swap loses castling rights.
* A rook moved by Royal Command loses castling rights.
* A king moved by Royal Escape loses castling rights.
* Returning a piece to its original square never restores lost rights.

## 25. En passant and magic

En passant is created only by a qualifying normal two-square pawn advance.

Magical movement does not create en passant eligibility.

An existing en passant opportunity expires after the opponent's next completed action, even if that action is magical.

## 26. Promotion and magic

Normal promotion rules apply only when a pawn reaches its promotion rank through a legal normal move.

Magical relocation cannot directly trigger promotion.

Any magic that changes a pawn's position must correctly update movement history.

## 27. Fifty-move and seventy-five-move counters

Use a consistent definition for Arcane Chess.

A pawn movement resets the relevant halfmove counter, whether that pawn was moved normally or magically.

A normal capture resets the counter.

A magical action involving only non-pawn pieces does not reset the counter.

This rule must be documented clearly.

## 28. Repetition detection

A position in Arcane Chess is determined by more than the placement of pieces.

Position identity must account for:

* Piece placement.
* Side to move.
* Relevant castling rights.
* Relevant en passant rights.
* White mana.
* Black mana.
* White Royal Escape availability.
* Black Royal Escape availability.

Movement-history details must be represented wherever they affect legal future actions.

Two boards with identical pieces but different mana values are not the same Arcane Chess position.

Use a reliable hashing system.

Include collision-resistant verification where practical.

## 29. Dead positions

Be conservative when declaring automatic dead-position draws in Arcane Chess.

Some positions that are dead in traditional chess may behave differently when magical repositioning is available.

Do not blindly reuse traditional insufficient-material shortcuts.

At minimum, recognize trivially dead positions such as king versus king.

Other cases should be recognized only when proven safe.

## 30. Stalemate

Stalemate occurs when the player to move:

* Is not in check.
* Has no legal normal chess move.
* Has no legal magical action.

The legal-action check must use the player's actual mana after turn-start regeneration.

Royal Escape is unavailable when the king is not in check.

## 31. Deterministic game state

Every action must be replayable.

For a given complete state and action, the resulting state must always be identical.

Avoid hidden gameplay randomness.

Use randomness only for cosmetic effects or controlled AI difficulty variation.

The deterministic rules engine will enable testing, replays, AI search, and debugging.

# PART IX — STRATEGIC DEPTH AND BALANCE

## 32. Core balance goals

The game must encourage:

* Positional planning.
* Tactical foresight.
* Mana conservation.
* Defensive resource management.
* Long-term coordination.
* Calculated sacrifices.
* King safety.
* Piece development.
* Timing.
* Multi-turn ability combinations.

The magic system should expand chess rather than replace it.

A player who plays strategically should consistently outperform a player who wastes mana.

## 33. No dominant ability

Avoid a situation where one ability becomes the universally correct choice.

Every ability must have meaningful strengths and weaknesses.

Examples:

Shadow Step should be useful for pawn structures.

Phase Shift should be useful for tactical repositioning.

Ghost Walk should be useful for escaping blocked diagonals.

Castle Swap should be useful for rearranging supporting pieces.

Royal Command should be useful for coordinated positional play.

Royal Escape should be useful as an expensive emergency defense.

These are intended strategic roles, not assumptions that the initial parameters are perfectly balanced.

## 34. Avoid positive feedback loops

Do not award extra mana for captures or material advantage.

Do not make the leading player automatically regenerate resources faster.

Do not allow permanent upgrades to grant competitive advantages in normal Arcane Chess matches.

Both sides must have access to the same abilities under the same rules.

## 35. Magical combinations

Create strategic combinations naturally through the rules.

Examples:

* Royal Command repositioning a knight to threaten a fork.
* Ghost Walk opening a diagonal attack.
* Shadow Step changing a pawn structure to expose a file.
* Castle Swap creating a discovered attack.
* Phase Shift creating pressure that forces a defensive response.
* Conserving mana to retain Royal Escape as a defensive option.

Do not give players free additional actions for executing combinations.

The combination system should reward planning over several turns.

| Ghost Walk    |         3 |

# PART X — THE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE ENGINE

## 37. General AI philosophy

The AI is one of the most important components of the game.

Do not implement a random-move opponent.

Do not create AI that blindly captures the highest-value available piece.

Do not create AI that activates magic whenever mana is available.

Implement a genuine chess search engine capable of evaluating positions and planning ahead.

The AI must be able to play both Classic Chess and Arcane Chess.

It must use the same legal move generator as the human player.

The AI must not cheat.

## 38. Search algorithm

Implement an efficient search system appropriate for the GBA.

Preferred techniques:

* Negamax.
* Alpha-beta pruning.
* Iterative deepening.
* Principal variation move ordering.
* Transposition tables.
* Zobrist hashing.
* Capture ordering.
* Killer move heuristics.
* History heuristics.
* Quiescence search.
* Check extensions where safe.
* Incremental evaluation where appropriate.

Advanced pruning techniques may be added if they are carefully validated.

Avoid unsafe pruning assumptions that ignore magical tactical moves.

For example, a quiet magical repositioning action may create a serious check or tactical threat.

The search system must understand that a move without a capture can still be extremely important.

## 39. Search correctness

The AI must consider:

* Standard legal chess moves.
* All affordable legal magical abilities.
* The opponent's mana.
* The opponent's special abilities.
* Royal Escape availability.
* Checkmate.
* Draw.
* Promotion.
* Repetition.
* Endgame situations.

Do not generate magical moves in a separate tactical subsystem that the main search ignores.

Normal moves and magic are alternative actions in the same search tree.

## 40. Position evaluation

Begin with conventional approximate piece values:

| Piece  | Base evaluation |
| ------ | --------------: |
| Pawn   |             100 |
| Knight |             320 |
| Bishop |             330 |
| Rook   |             500 |
| Queen  |             900 |

These are starting weights, not absolute truths.

The evaluation function should also consider:

* Piece-square tables.
* Mobility.
* King safety.
* Pawn structure.
* Passed pawns.
* Isolated pawns.
* Doubled pawns.
* Open files.
* Bishop activity.
* Knight outposts.
* Material balance.
* Development.
* Tactical threats.
* Endgame phase.
* Mana reserves.
* Ability availability.
* Royal Escape status.
* Vulnerability to magical repositioning.

Avoid simplistic evaluation that values six mana at a fixed price regardless of the position.

Mana can be more valuable when tactical opportunities or defensive emergencies exist.

Its marginal value should also reflect the six-point storage cap.

## 41. Quiescence search

Use quiescence search to reduce tactical instability near the search horizon.

The implementation must remain aware of checks and forced responses.

When a position is in check, do not use a simplistic stand-pat evaluation as though the side could safely do nothing.

Generate all necessary legal evasions.

Consider a carefully bounded selection of forcing magical actions where required for tactical correctness.

Do not allow unbounded quiescence expansion.

## 42. Time and computation budgets

The original GBA has limited processing power.

The AI must respect a configurable computation budget.

Use iterative deepening so that a valid best-known move is always available.

If the budget expires, return the best completed search result.

Do not select a partially evaluated illegal move.

Do not freeze the console indefinitely.

The computer's thinking must be interruptible by appropriate game-state controls.

### Interface during thinking

Display:

* A thinking indicator.
* Subtle character animation.
* An animated magical effect.
* Optional indication of current search progress.

The board and essential UI should continue to update.

Avoid misleading indicators that imply search depth or certainty not actually achieved.

Difficulty must not be implemented by allowing illegal moves or granting secret mana.

## 44. AI personalities

# PART XIII — COMBAT ANIMATION SYSTEM

## 51. Combat presentation philosophy

Combat animations should evoke high-quality tactical RPGs from the Game Boy Advance era.

When a piece captures another piece, the game should be capable of transitioning from the strategic board to a dramatic side-view combat scene.

The attacking and defending pieces appear as fantasy characters.

The attack is shown through animation.

The defending character is defeated.

The game returns to the updated chessboard.

These animations are visualizations of deterministic chess outcomes.

They must not introduce additional combat calculations.

There are no HP bars.

There are no random misses.

There are no additional damage mechanics.

## 52. Combat scene composition

The battle screen should use a side-view layout.

The attacking character occupies one side.

The defending character occupies the opposite side.

A themed background provides context.

Use:

* Animated character sprites.

* Camera movement or simulated camera movement.
* Screen shake.
* Particle effects.
