# Art direction

## Style

Classic Game Boy Advance pixel art: crisp 16-color sprites and backgrounds on the GBA's 240x160 screen, framed
by gold-trimmed windows on midnight blue. The six kingdoms each have a color identity (emerald forest, ember
fortress, frozen palace, umbral library, storm citadel, the ruined crown nexus), used on the world map, the
backdrops, the boards, the dark armies and the boss portraits. Chess comes first: the board is high-contrast and
legible, the pieces are distinct at 16x16, and magic adds light, smoke and motion without hiding the position.

Every graphic is **original** and generated by code (see "Production"). There are no third-party assets.

## Palettes

* Everything is 4 bits per pixel: 16 colors per palette, index 0 transparent.
* **Armies**: one piece sheet, recolored by palette. Palette indices have fixed roles (outline, four body shades,
  three accent shades, two secondary, detail, gem, rim), so the light army, the obsidian dark army and the six
  kingdom armies are palette swaps of the same art, and the battle characters use the same roles. Light factions
  keep light bodies and dark factions dark bodies, so the two sides of a match always stay distinguishable.
* **Boards**: 8 themes (Royal, Verdant, Inferno, Frost, Umbral, Storm, Nexus and a high-contrast theme for
  accessibility), each a palette of the same board tiles.
* **Text**: white, gold, gray, red, cyan, dark (small font); gold, silver, ruby, arcane, dim (title font, with a
  vertical metallic gradient and an outline).
* **Tints** for spell effects: a shadow silhouette with a glowing violet outline (Shadow Step), a white flash (Phase
  Shift) and a golden glow (Castle Swap, Royal Command, Royal Escape) replace a piece's palette for a moment.
* A dedicated palette for captured dark pieces in the side panel adds a light outline on the dark panel.

## Animation conventions

* 60 frames per second; animations count frames.
* **Board moves** slide over 14 frames (full), 9 (fast) or 5 (minimal), or are instant (off). Spells last 48 frames
  (full) or 28 (fast). Each spell moves its piece in its own part of the spell, with easing:
  * Shadow Step: smoke clouds, a shadow silhouette shifting sideways, the smoke dispersing.
  * Phase Shift: flashes, an afterimage, a flare at the destination, the knight reappearing in a ring of light.
  * Ghost Walk: translucent inside a spectral aura, passing through the friendly piece, rematerializing.
  * Castle Swap: an energy line between the pieces, both glowing gold, crossing arcs, shockwave rings.
  * Royal Command: a rune above the queen, an energy line to the ally, the ally's move, a flare.
  * Royal Escape: the screen darkens, a crown seal appears, the king flashes gold, leaps in an arc, and the seal
    breaks into fragments.
* **Duels** (captures) are timelines of keys at frame offsets: pose changes, runs, white flashes, screen shakes, a
  5-frame hit-stop at the impact, sounds, particle bursts, 16x16 effects, a spell circle, projectiles, fades and
  falls (a falling figure pivots around its feet). Sequence: entrance with a camera pan (28 frames), the attacker's
  class-specific attack, the victim's defeat reaction, a victory pose, fade to black: 2 to 2.5 seconds in full
  mode, half that in fast mode (fast mode shows duels only for captures of pieces above a pawn and for the capture
  that ends the game). A or B skips at any time.
* **Reduced effects** (options) removes flashes, screen shakes and the darkening, and keeps the motion.
* Idle characters breathe (two frames, 18-frame period); running figures bob.

## Screen layout

* Match: the board on the left (8x8 squares of 16 px, coordinates on the frame), a side panel on the right (84 px of
  text: both sides with mana gems, and a middle view the R button cycles: piece and ability, threats, game summary),
  the status line on top and the hint line at the bottom.
* All text is checked by the validator against the real font metrics and the layout widths
  (`core/cl_text_layout.h`, `core/cl_ui_text.h`), so nothing overflows or uses a missing glyph.

## Production

The art is drawn by Python scripts with the standard library only (`assets_source/`): a small canvas with lines,
circles, ellipses, polygons, outlines and bevels (`assets_source/gfx/canvas.py`), BMP and PNG writers, and palette
definitions (`assets_source/gfx/palettes.py`). One script per family:

| Script | Output |
|---|---|
| `gen_fonts.py`, `gen_font_big.py` | the two fonts and their metrics headers |
| `gen_pieces.py`, `gen_board.py` | pieces, army palettes, board tiles and themes |
| `gen_ui.py`, `gen_icons.py` | panel tiles, cursor, banner, mini pieces, icons |
| `gen_portraits.py` | the 14 portraits |
| `gen_backdrops.py` | the 8 backdrops |
| `gen_effects.py` | particles, spell effects, sigils, crown, map marker, tint palettes |
| `gen_battle.py` | the battle characters, floor and shadow |
| `gen_logo.py` | the title logo |

The scripts are deterministic: running `py -3.12 assets_source/generate_all.py` reproduces every file byte for
byte. Previews for review are written to `assets_source/previews/`. Butano converts the BMP files (with their JSON
descriptions) into ROM data at build time.
